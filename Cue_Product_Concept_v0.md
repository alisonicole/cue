_Alison's working perspective as of September 22, 2026._

## The concept, in one paragraph

Cue is a phone-first app that combines three surfaces into a single dopamine-safe alternative to doom-scrolling. A **live, curated community feed** you can open all day like Instagram, but that leaves you _better_ than before. A **values-anchored journal** that keeps you oriented to what actually matters — with a visibility slider on every entry so you decide what stays private and what becomes a community post. And a **quiet pattern layer underneath** that notices what you can't see about yourself — emotional patterns, values drift, cycle correlations — and gently surfaces them when helpful, never as judgment or score.

**One-line positioning**: _we replace the app you're doom-scrolling with one that leaves you better than you started._

---

## The three surfaces

### 1. The Feed (open me anytime)

The primary surface. What a user sees when she taps the icon.

- **Community posts** from other women in Cue — thoughts, questions, small wins, hard moments (semi-real identity: display name + optional photo, per-post visibility control)
- **Curated learning content** in **Instagram carousel format** — 8 slides, one idea, ADHD-attention-safe. No long-form modules ever.
- **Guest voices** — Michelle, guest clinicians, researchers, lived-experience contributors
- **Weekly rituals** — recurring themed threads ("Sunday Small Wins," "Wednesday What-Ifs")
- **Body-doubling drop-ins** shown when live rooms are open

**No infinite scroll, no algorithmic dark patterns.** The feed refreshes with real cadence (new posts throughout the day) but has a natural stopping point ("you're caught up — see you later").

**Personalization** comes from the values you set during onboarding + what you engage with, not from surveillance-grade behavioral modeling.

### 2. The Journal (values-anchored, spectrum-visibility)

Not diary journaling. Not homework. A place to notice, name, and hold what matters.

Every entry has a **visibility slider**: private → close friends → whole community → public. Nothing is ever "just" one thing.

**Entry types** (working guesses — Michelle will refine):

- **Values check-ins** — quick reflection tied to the values you set
- **Committed action logs** — small experiments you're trying this week
- **Noticing entries** — "here's what I'm feeling / observing right now"
- **Community-ready posts** — anything you write can become a post with one tap

**The pattern layer** works quietly across your entries: emotional patterns (words that repeat), values drift (are you spending time on what you said mattered?), cycle correlations (self-tagged, no biometric integration in v1), time-of-day patterns.

Patterns surface **gently, on request, or as opt-in weekly nudges** — never as scores, never as comparison, never as ranked leaderboards.

### 3. The Hard Moment button (always one tap away)

**The killer feature.** Dedicated always-visible button (bottom of screen? persistent widget?). Tap when you're spiraling, RSD'd, anxious, overwhelmed.

Scripted, evidence-based, 90-second flow:

1. **Acknowledge**: _"Yeah. That sounds hard. Can you tell me what's happening?"_ (present-moment awareness, ACT)
2. **Space**: user writes freely, no character limit, saved private by default
3. **Reframe**: _"If a friend of yours were feeling exactly this — what would you want her to know?"_ (self-compassion, Kristin Neff, evidence-based)
4. **Return**: user writes what she'd say to a friend
5. **Release**: _"Say that to yourself. Then close this. You don't have to do anything else."_

**Not AI therapy.** Not chatbot. A tiny, deliberate, Michelle-authored ritual grounded in ACT and self-compassion technique. Same script every time, so it becomes reliable.

Optional: after a Hard Moment, Cue asks if the user wants to share (visibility slider) with the community. This turns private hard moments into ambient community support without violating anyone's privacy.

## A day in the life

- **Morning (7:00–8:00 am)**: Cue widget on home screen shows one thing — a value the user named ("rest matters this week"), or a community post she'd like, or a small prompt. Optional gentle notification: _"One quiet moment before the scroll?"_ Tap in for 30 seconds, or don't.
- **Midday**: opens Cue instead of Instagram during a work break. Sees three community posts, engages with one. Scrolls a curated carousel about task initiation. Closes app. Feels better, not worse.
- **Afternoon (bad moment)**: RSD spike after a meeting. Taps the Hard Moment button. 90-second reflection. Optional share to community. Back to work.
- **Evening**: gentle nudge (opt-in): _"Anything you want to notice from today?"_ 30-second reflection. Or skip.

## A week in the life

- **Sunday morning**: 5-minute values check-in. What mattered this week? What do you want to move toward next week? One small experiment to try.
- **Throughout the week**: the feed, the community, the ambient cues, the Hard Moment button when needed.
- **Friday evening**: weekly email digest — "your Cue week-in-review." Patterns Cue noticed. Posts from your community you might have missed. One thoughtful piece Michelle wrote or curated. Substack-shaped, warm, opt-out anytime.
- **Monthly**: longer "state of you" moment — 10 minutes of guided reflection with option to share highlights to community.

## Content model

What lives in the feed:

- **Community posts** (user-generated, moderated) — 60% of content
- **Curated Instagram carousels** (8-slide, one-idea, from Cue team + guest voices) — 25% of content
- **Guest voice essays and audio** (Michelle, invited clinicians, researchers, lived-experience) — 10% of content
- **Weekly ritual threads** (recurring themed prompts) — 5% of content

**Explicit no-go**: infinite text, sequenced modules, videos over 60 seconds without captions, anything that requires sustained reading attention, anything that reads as homework.

**Explicit yes**: interactive carousels, short videos with captions, illustrated essays, curated "questions of the week," ACT micro-practices as illustrated 3-minute reads.

## Notifications & reminders (positive-definition, not "not annoying")

Locked from user (Alison) instinct:

- **Morning reminder** — one gentle notification with something meaningful (a value, a post, a prompt). Better than starting the day on IG. Opt-in during onboarding; user chooses time.
- **Home screen widget** — one piece of dynamic content: today's value, one community post, or Cue's noticing of the moment
- **Weekly email digest** — Friday-ish. "Your Cue week-in-review." Substack-shaped: warm, non-urgent, reads like a friend catching you up.
- **Email pushes for posts you'd like** — Substack model. Not "you got a like." Instead: "This post you'd probably love went up today." Curated by relevance to your values / engagement.

**Never**:

- Red badge counters
- Streaks or day-count reminders
- "You haven't opened Cue in X days" nagging
- Push notifications for engagement metrics (likes, follows, replies)
- Any notification that leaves the user worse than before receiving it

## Onboarding flow

Three parts, in order, spread over the first several days:

**Part 1: Why this exists (~2 min)**

- 4–6 slides that name why Cue was built, who it's for, what it isn't
- The Hamdani premise front and center
- Michelle + Alison introduction as founders
- Ends with: "This isn't an app you finish. It's a place you show up. Ready to set your values?"

**Part 2: Your values (~5–10 min — mechanism TBD with Michelle)**

- Values setup — approach TBD. Three candidate mechanisms:
  - Menu of ~40 values, pick 3–5 (ACT-style Values Card Sort)
  - Guided prompts that surface values in her own language
  - Combination — starter menu + editable
- **Explicit note**: this is Michelle's design decision. Her ACT training will drive this.

**Part 3: The community (~2 min then ongoing)**

- Land in the feed
- Introduced to the community with a soft "you're new here, take your time" onboarding post
- Optional first post prompt (skippable): "hi, I'm here"
- Introduced to the Hard Moment button explicitly

## Semi-real identity, in practice

- Display name: user chooses, can be real or pseudonymous
- Photo: optional at any time
- Per-post visibility: private / close friends / whole community / public — slider on every entry and post
- Following model: TBD (Reddit-style community rooms with no follow, or Substack-style follow specific voices — worth exploring both with Michelle)
- Direct messages: **NO in v1**. Community-only interaction. Reduces safety burden, keeps focus on shared space.

## What Cue is NOT (the not-doing list, product edition)

- Not a therapy replacement
- Not an AI chatbot or AI therapist
- Not a diagnostic tool
- Not a habit tracker with streaks
- Not a sequenced course platform
- Not a coaching marketplace
- Not a medication funnel
- Not gamified
- Not surveillance-flavored (no comparison scores, no leaderboards, no "your ADHD grade")
- Not a place for men (v1)

## What's locked vs. what needs Michelle

### Locked from user research + Alison's product instinct

- Three-surface architecture (feed + journal + hard-moment)
- Semi-real identity with per-post visibility slider
- Onboarding story → values → community sequence
- No long-form modules, Instagram carousel content format
- Text-based emotional pattern layer (no biometrics in v1)
- Journal ↔ community as spectrum, not wall

### Needs Michelle's input to lock

- **Values setup mechanism** — menu vs. guided vs. hybrid. Her ACT training decides this.
- **Hard Moment script exact language** — Alison sketched it; Michelle authors the real thing.
- **Pattern layer surfacing rules** — how often, in what voice, with what caveats. ACT lens on "compassionate noticing" vs. judgment matters here.
- **Values check-in cadence and depth** — weekly? Daily-optional? What's the ritual?
- **Content curation editorial voice** — what makes a carousel "Cue voice"? Where does Michelle's writing appear?
- **Community moderation posture** — what topics are okay, what triggers a moderator flag, how do we handle ABA discussions, medication mentions, diagnosis disclosures

### Open questions we should sit with together

- **Follow model**: subreddit-style (community rooms, no follows) vs. Substack-style (follow specific voices)?
- **Content mix over time**: is 60/25/10/5 the right ratio? How does it evolve?
- **Pattern layer depth**: how much does Cue "notice" in v1 vs. Wave 2?
- **Bad-moment aftercare**: is there a follow-up ping the next day, or do we respect that she said "close this"?

## Why this concept is defensible

1. **Community you can't get elsewhere** — no other product has moderated, curated, sensory-safe ADHD-women community as a first-class surface
2. **Longitudinal self-data** — a year of your values, moods, patterns is a genuine switching cost
3. **Pattern intelligence** — AI helps _without_ being an AI therapist (finding correlations in your own data ≠ interpreting your feelings)
4. **ACT + self-compassion clinical spine** — Michelle's actual toolkit, shipped as product
5. **The Hard Moment button** — a killer feature nobody else has, cheap to build, high emotional stakes, hard to copy without the clinical authorship
6. **Anti-doom-scroll positioning** — we're not competing with Inflow. We're competing with Instagram.
