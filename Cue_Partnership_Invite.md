## What's evolved since the v1 you saw

The core idea (a warm, women-first support tool for ADHD) is intact. Since v1, three things have shifted, and I want your gut on all of them:

1. **ADHD-first, AuDHD-inclusive by design (not "for autistic women").** Roughly 40% of autistic adults also meet ADHD criteria — and among AuDHD adults, 71% are women. So AuDHD women are absolutely in the audience. But neither of us has autism lived experience, and you don't specialize in autism clinically. Building _for_ autism (as a co-primary audience) would be overreach. Better: build for ADHD women, design in a way that welcomes AuDHD, refer autism-only women to autism-specialized resources. Honest scope.

2. **From cognitive-therapy-adjacent to ACT + executive-function scaffolding as the spine.** ACT (Acceptance and Commitment Therapy) is where your work lives, and I think it's actually the most distinctive positioning we could have. Values-based, acceptance-focused, third-wave — not "fix your brain" and not "manage your symptoms." Combined with executive-function scaffolding (environment as commitment device), we get: _"we help you notice what your brain does, accept it, and design your life around what actually matters to you."_

3. **From therapist-facing to consumer-first, with community as the front door.** The therapist model was clean but slow. Consumer-first is faster, bigger, closer to where women are already looking for help (Reddit, TikTok, Kaleidoscope Society). Community isn't a bolt-on feature — it's the entry point.

## The mission (I feel strongly about this)

**Cue exists to help women with ADHD stop white-knuckling their lives — by giving them tools grounded in behavioral science and ACT, environments that fit how their brains actually work, and a community of women who get it.**

## The premise, in one quote

> _"Every time you rely on your memory to hold something in your brain, you're spending executive function that you simply cannot spare."_
> — Sasha Hamdani, MD

This is the scientific thesis of Cue in one line. ADHD isn't a willpower problem, it's an executive-function economy operating under scarcity. Every cognitive load we ask an ADHD brain to carry is a withdrawal from an account already overdrawn. The ACT layer adds the values compass: what do we _want_ to spend our spared executive function on? Not everything is worth doing, and Cue helps women get clear on which "everythings" are.

Every product decision has to pass one test: **does it spend a user's executive function, or save it — in service of what she says matters most?**

## Three things I want your gut on before we're too far in

I've been sitting with the strategy brief for weeks. I don't want to lead you through my thinking before I hear your unfiltered reaction. In order of importance:

**1. Does ACT + executive-function scaffolding feel like the right spine?**
You live in ACT. Does the framing feel authentic to how you'd want to design tools, or am I stretching it? If ACT is the core, I want your voice defining what that looks like in-product — small values-based prompts, defusion techniques, committed-action nudges. I have opinions but I don't have your training.

**2. What's your instinct on scope — ADHD-first with AuDHD-inclusive design, or something else?**
I've walked myself back from a bigger neurodivergent umbrella because neither of us has autism lived experience or specialization. But you know your caseload better than I do. If AuDHD is a bigger share of your clients than I'm assuming, that shifts things. And if you think there's an autism-adjacent piece we should include from day one, I want to hear it.

**3. Can you credibly bring 10–15 women from your clinical/research network into a private beta in the first 90 days?**
Not asking them to pay. Asking them to shape it. This is the highest-leverage growth lever we have in year one and it's not something I can generate. If yes, we have a real path. If no, we design a different first 90 days.

## Four places I've deliberately left open for you

The strategy brief has a lot in it — but these four surfaces I've held loosely on purpose, because they should be yours to design:

- **The ACT + behavioral curriculum** — what tools, what prompts, what psychoeducation, in what sequence. I have research collected; you have the ACT training and the intervention-design chops.
- **The community structure and moderation posture** — what makes an ADHD-women community actually healthy vs. what breaks it. I can build the software; you know the human dynamics.
- **The brand voice on clinical content** — language discipline (traits not symptoms, no "manage," no "high-functioning"), tone on hard topics like diagnosis and medication. You're closer to how women in these communities actually talk about themselves.
- **The "not doing" list** — what Cue refuses. I've drafted a starting list, but if you'd add or subtract anything, this is where I want your voice most.

## What I'm actually asking for on Wednesday

Not a signature. A **90-day co-creation sprint**.

Concretely, that means: for the next 90 days, we work together on Cue as if we're partners. Weekly working sessions. You lead on curriculum outline and community principles. I lead on landing page, waitlist, and technical scoping. We each contribute the modest cash needed (~$3–5K each in the sprint to cover legal setup, domain hosting, design tools).

At the end of 90 days, we decide together: formalize as co-founders (with real paperwork), or walk away clean, keeping the friendship intact and the work we've done as a shared reference for whatever either of us builds next.

**Why a 90-day sprint and not a full commitment right now**: neither of us has co-founded with the other before. This is the version of "yes" that respects that. If it works, we know. If it doesn't, we haven't burned each other or the idea.
