## What's evolved since the v1 you saw

The core idea (a warm, women-first support tool for ADHD) is intact. But three things have shifted, all in directions I think you'll like:

1. **From ADHD-only to neurodivergent women — ADHD, autism, AuDHD.** The research is unambiguous: ~40% of autistic adults also meet ADHD criteria, and among AuDHD adults, 71% are women. Building for ADHD alone would leave your whole AuDHD population underserved. Given how much this space matters to you, expanding the umbrella feels right.

2. **From "behavior-first" to "environment-first."** Same behavioral-science backbone, better positioning. "Environment-first" honors the anti-ABA sensibilities of the autism community without abandoning what behavioral science actually offers. It also sidesteps the cognitive-vs-behavioral schism you named. More on this below — this is one I want your gut check on.

3. **From therapist-facing to consumer-first, with community as the front door.** The therapist model was clean but slow. Consumer-first is faster, bigger, closer to where women are already looking for help (Reddit, TikTok, Kaleidoscope Society). Community isn't a bolt-on feature — it's the entry point.

## The mission (I feel strongly about this)

**Cue exists to help neurodivergent women stop white-knuckling their lives — by designing environments that fit how their brains actually work, and building community with women who get it.**

## The premise, in one quote

> _"Every time you rely on your memory to hold something in your brain, you're spending executive function that you simply cannot spare."_
> — Sasha Hamdani, MD

This is the scientific thesis of Cue in one line. ADHD and autism aren't willpower problems, they're executive function economies operating under scarcity. Every cognitive load we ask a neurodivergent woman to carry is a withdrawal from an account already overdrawn. Environment-first is the mathematical response: externalize the load, spare the executive function.

Every product decision has to pass one test: **does it spend a user's executive function, or save it?**

## Three things I want your gut on before we're too far in

I've written up the case for each of these in the full strategy brief, but I don't want to lead you there before hearing your unfiltered reaction. In order of importance:

**1. Does "environment-first" feel authentic to how you practice?**
Not "does it sound like a good marketing frame" — does it feel like a true statement about your clinical work? If it feels performative, we shouldn't build the brand on it. If it feels like a language you already use in different words, we've found something.

**2. How do you sit publicly on the ABA / neurodiversity spectrum?**
Autism community stakes are very different from ADHD community stakes. If we're building for AuDHD women, you'll be the public clinical voice — and every word will be scrutinized for ABA-adjacency. I don't need a formal position, but I need to know if you're comfortable being explicitly, publicly aligned with neurodiversity-affirming practice.

**3. Can you credibly bring 10–15 women from your clinical network into a private beta in the first 90 days?**
Not asking them to pay. Asking them to shape it. This is the highest-leverage growth lever we have in year one and it's not something I can generate. If yes, we have a real path. If no, we need to design a different first 90 days.

## Four places I've deliberately left open for you

The strategy brief I've been drafting has a lot in it — but these four surfaces I've held loosely on purpose, because they should be yours to design:

- **The clinical curriculum** — what behavioral tools, what environmental scaffolds, what psychoeducation, in what order. I have research collected; you have the practice wisdom.
- **The community structure and moderation posture** — what makes an ND-women community actually healthy vs. what breaks it. I can build the software; you know the human dynamics.
- **The brand voice on clinical content** — language discipline (traits not symptoms, no "manage," no "high-functioning"), tone on hard topics like diagnosis, medication, ABA. You're closer to how women in these communities actually talk about themselves.
- **The "not doing" list** — what Cue refuses. I've drafted a starting list, but if you'd add or subtract anything, this is where I want your voice most.

## How I imagine us working together

Proposal, not decree. Everything below is a starting point.

**Roles**: 50/50 co-founders. You own clinical strategy, community, and public clinical voice. I own product, engineering direction, brand, distribution, ops, and fundraising if we go there. All major product decisions joint. All major clinical decisions yours to call.

**Time & shape**: both of us keep our day jobs in the near term. 10–15 hrs/week each, ramping as revenue supports it. No salaries in year 1. Cash contributions split evenly (~$25–50K each) for infrastructure, legal, contract engineering.

**Equity**: 50/50, 4-year vesting, 1-year cliff. IP fully assigned to the company.

**The two rules I'd want us to hold each other to**: (1) any major clinical decision I disagree with, I defer to you. Any major product/business decision you disagree with, you defer to me. (2) On the "not doing" list, both of us need to sign off on any change — that's our shared spine.

## What I'm actually asking for on Wednesday

Not a signature. A **90-day co-creation sprint**.

Concretely, that means: for the next 90 days, we work together on Cue as if we're partners. Weekly working sessions. You lead on curriculum outline and community principles. I lead on landing page, waitlist, and technical scoping. We each contribute the modest cash needed (~$3–5K each in the sprint to cover legal setup, domain hosting, design tools).

At the end of 90 days, we decide together: formalize as co-founders (with real paperwork), or walk away clean, keeping the friendship intact and the work we've done as a shared reference for whatever either of us builds next.

**Why a 90-day sprint and not a full commitment right now**: neither of us has co-founded with the other before. This is the version of "yes" that respects that. If it works, we know. If it doesn't, we haven't burned each other or the idea.
